## The gap between building fast and building right

Minimum Viable Product changed how we build software.

Instead of waiting for completeness, we learned to ship early, learn quickly, and iterate. That shift unlocked speed across the industry. It reduced risk. It made progress visible.

But it also relied on something that is no longer guaranteed.

<!-- truncate -->

It assumed that the people building the product held enough context to make good decisions.

That assumption is now breaking.

---

## Speed is no longer the constraint

AI has fundamentally changed execution.

Individuals can now generate working code, content, and even full systems in hours. Small teams can operate with the leverage of much larger organizations. Multi-agent systems can design, build, test, and refine work in parallel.

Execution is no longer scarce.

Direction is.

The challenge is no longer “how fast can we build?”  
It is “are we building the right thing, for the right reason, under the right constraints?”

That is a context problem.

---

## What is Minimum Viable Context?

Minimum Viable Context (MVC) is the smallest set of shared understanding required to make a good decision.

Not perfect understanding. Not complete documentation. Just enough clarity to avoid building in the wrong direction.

At a minimum, that context should answer four questions:

- What outcome are we trying to create?
- Who is this for?
- What constraints must we respect?
- How will we know if it worked?

If those answers are unclear, everything that follows becomes unstable. Work may still get done, but it drifts. It optimizes locally while missing the broader outcome.

---

## MVP assumed context. AI exposes its absence

When MVP was introduced, the constraint was development speed. Teams needed a way to reduce scope and learn faster.

But even then, the thinking behind the product was rarely minimal. Product managers, engineers, and leaders carried context through experience, conversations, and shared understanding.

Today, that implicit context is no longer enough.

AI systems do not infer intent the way humans do. They respond to what is provided. When context is thin, the output may still look correct, but it is often misaligned.

And because AI accelerates execution, that misalignment compounds quickly.

---

## When context is weak, speed amplifies the wrong things

Many organizations are already seeing this pattern.

Despite heavy investment in AI, many organizations report little measurable improvement in revenue or cost outcomes. In many cases, teams can point to time saved or activity increased, but struggle to connect those gains to business impact.

This is not a tooling problem. It is a context problem.

Saving time only creates value if that time is redirected toward meaningful outcomes. Without clarity on what those outcomes are, efficiency becomes disconnected from impact.

Speed, on its own, does not create advantage. It exposes whether direction was ever clear.

---

## MVC comes before MVP

MVP asks a useful question:

What is the smallest thing we can build to learn?

MVC asks a more foundational one:

What is the smallest understanding we need before we build anything?

These are not competing ideas. They are sequential.

MVC defines direction.  
MVP tests direction.

Without MVC, MVP becomes an optimization loop around unclear goals. Teams move quickly, but not necessarily meaningfully.

---

## How MVC relates to MMF and other “minimums”

Over time, the industry has developed a pattern of reducing scope:

- MVP (Minimum Viable Product)
- MMF (Minimum Marketable Feature)
- MLP (Minimum Lovable Product)

Each of these focuses on output. They help teams decide what to build and how to release it.

MVC focuses on input.

It ensures that what is being built has a clear purpose, a defined audience, and a measurable outcome. Without that, the other “minimums” risk optimizing for delivery rather than value.

---

## The rise of agentic execution changes the stakes

The shift becomes more pronounced with multi-agent systems.

A single individual can now orchestrate multiple agents to:

- Generate architectures
- Write and refactor code
- Create tests and documentation
- Deploy and monitor systems

This is a force multiplier. But it is also a risk multiplier.

When multiple agents operate in parallel, they amplify whatever context they are given. If the direction is clear, progress accelerates. If it is not, divergence happens faster and at greater scale.

The bottleneck moves entirely upstream.

From execution  
To intent

---

## Common objections and where they break down

### “We will figure it out as we go”

Iteration is still valuable. But iteration assumes that feedback loops are meaningful.

When direction is unclear, iteration produces variation, not learning. Teams can explore multiple paths quickly without understanding which one actually matters.

Faster iteration does not replace clarity. It depends on it.

---

### “Context emerges from usage”

In some cases, yes. Real-world usage can reveal gaps and opportunities that were not visible upfront.

But AI systems are highly sensitive to initial framing. Early assumptions shape outputs, recommendations, and even downstream decisions. Poor context at the start does not just delay learning. It biases it.

---

### “More context will slow us down”

Too much context does slow teams down. That is why the focus is on minimum viable context.

The goal is not completeness. It is sufficiency.

Enough clarity to move with confidence.  
Not so much detail that progress stalls.

---

## A practical model for Minimum Viable Context

MVC does not require heavy process or documentation. It requires discipline.

Start with four anchors:

**Outcome**  
What changes if this works? Tie this to a real business or user impact.

**Actor**  
Who benefits or is affected? Be specific.

**Constraint**  
What must remain true? This may include cost, risk, compliance, or time boundaries.

**Signal**  
How will we know it is working? Define observable indicators, not just activity.

If these cannot be answered clearly and quickly, the work is not ready to scale.

---

## What this changes in practice

For developers, the shift is subtle but important. The question is no longer just “what should I build?” but “what context am I operating within?”

For product teams, it means reducing backlog volume and increasing clarity of intent. Fewer items. Better framing.

For leadership, it means funding outcomes rather than ideas. Clear expectations of impact, not just delivery.

For platform and internal developer portal teams, it introduces a new responsibility. Not just surfacing tools and services, but capturing, structuring, and distributing context so teams can act with clarity.

---

## Final thought

MVP made it safe to build small.

MVC makes it safe to build fast.

In a world where execution is abundant, context becomes the differentiator. Teams that define it well will compound value. Teams that do not will simply move faster in the wrong direction.
